Concatenate Deployments

oceanarray concat joins the stacks of one site’s deployments (e.g. egM2 2016–2018 and 2018–2020) end to end on one time axis. Each deployment keeps its own stage files, stack, grid and report; the concatenated stack (<site>_stackcat.nc) and its page (<site>_stackcat_report.html) are a layer on top.

oceanarray concat egM2_2016 egM2_2018 --name egM2 --proc-dir /data/proc

import oceanarray as oa

site = oa.concat([stack_2016, stack_2018], name="egM2")

What it does

  • Every level of every deployment is kept. A level is one instrument (or one ADCP bin) in one deployment. Serials repeat across deployments, so level_id (<deployment>/<serial>) names each level.

  • Stored by depth. The time series are on (time, N_DEPTHS): a depth holds the levels at about the same depth across deployments (see Depths), so one depth is one column through the years. level_index(time, N_DEPTHS) names the level behind each value (−1: none); a depth is empty (NaN, QC flag 9) outside its levels’ deployments.

  • Nothing is filled across a turnaround. The steps between two deployments stay empty; deployment_index(time) is −1 there.

  • Overlaps lose nothing. Where two deployments are in the water at once, the newer one fills the depth (overlap="older" for the other way round) and the other’s values go to the overlap block on N_OVERLAP: overlap_time, overlap_depth_index, overlap_level_index and overlap_<variable>.

  • One time step. The stacks must share stack: dt_seconds and sit on whole steps (any stack made by oceanarray 0.6 or later); otherwise concat refuses.

  • A record per deployment on the DEPLOYMENT dimension: name, deployment and recovery time, position, water depth, cruises and ships, the source stack’s file name and SHA-256, its history, the settings that built it (deployment_settings, YAML) and its people. A stack attribute the deployments agree on is kept once; one that differs becomes a deployment_<name> variable.

  • People. principal_investigator* and contributor_* (OceanSITES 1.4: commas between PIs, semicolons between contributors, one entry per person and role in every parallel attribute) are the union of every deployment’s, without repeats; a contributor with two roles has two entries. See Output files.

  • Memory. Each variable of each stack is read once and placed by integer slicing, so the peak is about the result plus one variable of one stack. The file is chunked by about a year along time.

Depths

A level joins the nearest depth of its family (hydrography: MicroCATs, temperature loggers and CTDs; point velocity: Aquadopps and current meters; ADCP bins; ADCP heads, which never share a depth with a bin) within the depth tolerance of that depth’s center (the mean of its levels so far), at most one level per deployment; otherwise it opens a new depth. The tolerance is depth_tolerance_m (default 50 m), or half the bin size for ADCP bins. A level that ends up farther than the tolerance from its depth’s final center is named in a warning.

Warning

Depth indices are not stable. Adding a deployment or changing the matching rule renumbers them, so nothing should read an index as a depth: a depth’s value is nominal_depth (the mean nominal depth of its levels). The report names each depth by its value.

The rule is set in each mooring YAML’s stack: block and must agree across the deployments:

stack:
  dt_seconds: 3600
  depth_match: depth        # depth (water depth − hab) or hab; default depth
  depth_tolerance_m: 50     # default 50

The report

The concatenated page mirrors the stack page: a Deployments table (each linking to its own stack report), the processing history (the concat step, then each deployment’s), the instruments by depth (each serial linking to its instrument page), hydrography and velocity with the turnarounds shaded, trajectories and current speed per depth, T-S diagrams colored by deployment, and current roses with one outline per deployment. Tilt, instrument spacing and the clock check stay in each deployment’s own report.

The links look for each deployment’s reports in the report folder beside its stack, or in <DIR>/<deployment> with --report-dir DIR (report_dir= in Python).

The site grid

oceanarray concat then grids the Stack cat onto pressure levels with the same gridder as a deployment’s grid (oceanarray.grid), one mooring per profile: at each step only the levels of the deployment deployment_index names are interpolated, so a profile never mixes two moorings; the overlap block is not gridded and turnarounds stay empty. The file, <site>_gridcat.nc, keeps the stack’s time step, deployment_index and the DEPLOYMENT record. The settings come from the call (--pmin, --pmax, --dp, --mask-flags), not from the deployments’ YAMLs, and are recorded in the file.

The site stack takes the deployments’ mask_flags rule; when they differ, --mask-flags must give the site’s (each deployment’s own stays in deployment_stack_mask_flags). A deployment without the originator’s flags reads 0 where it has values. Before gridding, values the rule hides are left out (default 4; velocity=3,4 adds suspect velocity). The Grid cat page rotates the velocity to --rotate-to (not recorded in the file), else to the direction of the record-mean, depth-mean velocity, used only when the flow is steady enough to have one (the speed of the mean daily velocity over the mean daily speed at least 0.3; otherwise no rotated panels, and a warning). The pressure range is --pmin / --pmax, else from the 5th percentile of the shallowest depth’s pressure to the 95th of the deepest’s, so an instrument riding above or below its usual depth stays on the grid. Each deployment’s own grid: settings are not used; a deployment whose settings differ is named in a warning. Current speed and direction are computed from the gridded east and north velocity.

Its page, <site>_gridcat_report.html (the “Grid cat” pill beside “Stack cat”), shows pressure × time sections of temperature and salinity with σ0 and σ2 isopycnals, their anomalies from the whole-record median at each level, the median profile of each deployment, the velocity sections (east, north, and the rotated pair, cropped to the pressures with velocity data at the same dbar per inch as the T and S sections) and a T-S diagram of the median profiles. The sections are averaged for display (daily for a record under a year, weekly under 3, 2-weekly under 20, monthly beyond), in bins that restart at each deployment’s start, so no bin spans two deployments and a step between them shows as a sharp edge; the file itself is not averaged.

RAPID

RAPID gridded each deployment first and then interpolated the segments onto one time axis, filling short gaps between deployments; oceanarray joins the stacks and fills nothing. See RAPID heritage.
