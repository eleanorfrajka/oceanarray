Calibration dips

A calibration dip (caldip) checks moored instruments against the ship’s CTD. The instruments are strapped to the CTD frame and lowered on a cast, before deployment and again after recovery. On the way up the CTD stops at several depths for 5–10 minutes, longer than a usual bottle stop, so the instruments (sampling every 10 s) and the CTD (24 Hz) settle at the same water.

How a stop is compared

  1. Find the stop from the bottle file: the CTD pressure stays within 5 dbar of the stop’s pressure (the mean of its first minute).

  2. Drop the last 30 s and keep the 3 minutes before them.

  3. In that window, take each instrument’s difference from the CTD in temperature, conductivity and (if it has a sensor) pressure, and the scatter of both. An instrument without a pressure sensor uses the CTD’s pressure for its conductivity.

The clocks are matched first by the moment the package hits the water, when the instruments were already sampling at the start of the cast.

What it is used for

  • Before deployment — an instrument that differs from the CTD by more than 0.005 °C, 0.05 mS cm⁻¹ or 5 dbar is not deployed.

  • Before and after — the differences at the two dips give each instrument’s offset and its drift over the deployment, for example a linear change in temperature between them.

The comparison is done by the caldip package (pip install git+https://github.com/ocean-uhh/caldip.git), which finds the water impact and the stops, computes the offsets and draws the figures. oceanarray will apply its offsets at the start of stage 3; that step is designed but not applied yet (Calibration — applying caldip corrections at the front of Stage 3).
